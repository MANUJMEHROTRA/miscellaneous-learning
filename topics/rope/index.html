01 / The idea

Position, encoded as a turn.

Attention needs to know not just what a token says, but where it sits. RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) injects position by rotating pairs of query and key features by position-dependent angles.

A

Pair up features

Take two coordinates from a query or key vector. Treat them as a point on a plane.

q
B

Rotate by position

At token position p, rotate that point by p times this pair's frequency.

↻p · ω
C

Compare the turns

The query–key dot product now depends on their relative positions, not just their content.

R(m)TR(n) = R(n − m)
↳

Useful intuition: different feature pairs turn at different speeds. Fast turns help distinguish nearby positions; slow turns change gradually over longer distances.

03 / The mathematics

Why a rotation tells you about distance.

RoPE applies a different 2D rotation to every adjacent feature pair. Here is the two-dimensional version.

1. Build the rotation

For a pair of coordinates, position p rotates the vector through angle pω:

Rotation matrix Rp,ω = [ cos(pω)   −sin(pω)
sin(pω)    cos(pω) ]

2. Give each pair its own pace

With head dimension d, pair i uses frequency ωi. The standard inverse-frequency schedule is geometric:

RoPE frequencies ωi = 1 / θ2i/d,   θ = 10,000

Lower-index pairs have higher frequencies; higher-index pairs turn more slowly. Implementations may vary the base or scaling strategy.

THE KEY IDENTITY

↓   R(m)T = R(−m)
Only the relative offset remainsqTRn−mk

For a whole head, this relation holds pair by pair. The combined attention score mixes their different frequencies.

For a fixed pair, the score varies with relative distance Δ = n − m: score(Δ) = ‖q‖ ‖k‖ cos(Δω + φk − φq) This is why position is relative in the attention score, even though each vector is rotated using its absolute position.

04 / YaRN & extending context

Stretch carefully, not uniformly.

A model trained for a shorter context has only learned positional patterns over that range. Simply asking it to use much larger positions can push RoPE into unfamiliar phases. YaRN is one method for adapting RoPE to longer contexts without treating every frequency identically.

01

Original RoPE

Keep the training frequencies. At larger positions, rotations travel farther into phase patterns the model may not have learned.

No frequency changes
02

Linear interpolation

Divide positions—or equivalently all frequencies—by the extension factor. This stretches the learned range, but can blur useful short-distance distinctions.

All frequencies slowed equally
03

YaRN

Blend between interpolated and original frequencies across feature dimensions. The transition is designed to preserve some local detail while extending longer-range behavior.

A smooth, frequency-aware blend
SIMPLIFIED FREQUENCY BLEND
ω′i = (1 − ri) · ωi/s + ri · ωi

s is the context extension factor. ri moves smoothly from 0 to 1 across a transition band of frequency dimensions.

Important: production YaRN includes additional choices—such as how transition bounds are set and attention scaling. This lab's blend is a conceptual approximation, not a drop-in implementation or a claim that all models use the same settings.

05 / Training & inference

How does a model get a longer window?

A context limit is not just a slider at inference time. Positional encoding, training exposure, attention implementation, memory, and quality all matter.

01

Pretrain

Learn language and attention patterns on sequences drawn from a training distribution. The maximum training length is not always the same as the final advertised window.

→
02

Extend

Use positional scaling, continued pretraining, long-context fine-tuning, or a combination. Training examples should teach the model to use information across the longer span.

→
03

Serve

Configure inference for that range and provide enough memory/compute. Then evaluate retrieval, reasoning, and quality at different positions—not only whether tokens fit.

!
What about GPT and Gemini?

The exact architectures, positional schemes, training sequence lengths, and extension recipes for current GPT and Gemini models are generally not fully public. Do not assume they use RoPE or YaRN. The methods here describe published techniques and general ways long-context capability can be developed—not confirmed details of those product families.

KEEP IN MIND

A larger context window means more tokens can be processed. It does not guarantee that the model will attend to, retrieve, or reason equally well over every token in that window.
